## How to add an entry

1. Architectural decisions → write an **ADR** in `docs/04-adr/`, then add a one-line entry here
   pointing to it.
2. Product, scope, or policy decisions → add a row here directly.
3. Reversing a decision → add a **new row** referencing the old one. Never edit history.
4. A decision that turned out wrong is worth recording *as* wrong. The reasoning is the value, not
   the correctness.

## 2026-08-25 — Filing mechanics and the utility layer

| # | Decision | Rationale | Status |
|---|---|---|---|
| D037 | **No server-side submission to any government portal, by any mechanism** — no headless drivers, no request replay | Responsibility follows the signature; a wrongly filed instrument is unrecoverable; bulk filing invites dismissal of the platform as vexatious | Accepted → [ADR 0014](../04-adr/0014-assisted-filing-not-automated-submission.md) |
| D038 | **Never hold a citizen's government-portal credentials or intercept their OTP** | Every filing route requires an account, several require payment. Server-side filing means a credential vault for government identities — a breach consequence out of all proportion to the convenience | Accepted → [ADR 0014](../04-adr/0014-assisted-filing-not-automated-submission.md) |
| D039 | **No anti-detection or fingerprint-spoofing tooling against any government system** | A bot defence is the operator's answer. Circumvention is a different thing to defend, and it would taint every record the platform publishes | Accepted → [ADR 0014](../04-adr/0014-assisted-filing-not-automated-submission.md) |
| D040 | **Build a utility layer, on the condition that every surface routes back to an accountability action** | Reporting is episodic; an app opened twice a year is deleted. But a utility surface with no route back is a worse version of an app that already exists | Accepted → [series U](../02-product/14-civic-utility-features.md) |
| D041 | **Utility features are never measured by engagement** | Time-in-app is the metric that turns a civic record into a feed. The measures stay confirmed fixes, corroborations and instruments filed | Accepted |
| D042 | **Warranty alerts ship in an honest mode before the DLP source is obtained** — completion date plus the Court's five-to-ten-year expectation, both sourced | Two sourced facts beat one inferred one, and it is still more than any other product tells a citizen | Accepted → U2 |
| D043 | **Notification ceiling of five per user per week**, quiet hours 21:00–08:00, no re-engagement messages ever | The utility layer can generate unlimited messages. The budget is the control | Accepted → U9 §9 |

## 2026-09-25 — Collection moves to Cloud Run

| # | Decision | Rationale | Status |
|---|---|---|---|
| D051 | **Collection runs as a Cloud Run job in `asia-south1`, triggered by Cloud Scheduler.** The VM, its disk and its instance schedule are deleted | A job in that region reaches BMC, verified by running one. No disk, no machine to patch, no serial console to lose, and it costs nothing: about 1.5% of the free vCPU allowance and 2 of 3 free scheduler jobs. Cheaper and simpler at once | Accepted → [ADR 0016](../04-adr/0016-collection-as-a-cloud-run-job.md) |
| D052 | **Collect twice a day, 02:30 and 14:30 IST, not once** | Cloud Monitoring's absence conditions cap at 23h30m, so a daily schedule cannot be watched for a missed run without a false alarm every day. Two runs make the dead-man alert meaningful, and catch BMC's daytime edits | Accepted → ADR 0016 |
| D053 | **One command (`ingest all`) rather than per-schedule argument overrides** | Overrides need `run.jobs.runWithOverrides`, which `roles/run.invoker` does not grant. Widening the trigger's permissions in order to collect resolutions is the wrong trade | Accepted → ADR 0016 |
| D054 | **Alert by email on a failed execution, and on no success for 23h30m** | Until now a failure reached a log nobody reads, and a night that never ran was invisible. A missed night cannot be recovered | Accepted → ADR 0016 |

## 2026-09-25 — Parallel tracks, and the first backend slice

| # | Decision | Rationale | Status |
|---|---|---|---|
| D055 | **The roadmap runs as three parallel tracks — collection, backend, fieldwork — not as a serial sequence** | Collection now runs itself, and nothing about the backend waits on it or on fieldwork. The original ordering was written before the collector existed. A phase ends when the slowest track lands, and that is usually fieldwork, not code | Accepted → [roadmap](../05-delivery/01-roadmap.md) |
| D056 | **The capture endpoint stores the photograph before it replies** | A 202 promises the capture is safe. Returning before the image is in object storage would make that a lie, and hard rule 7 forbids losing a capture | Accepted → `internal/api` |
| D057 | **The field kit queues captures on the device and uploads when it can** | A walk through a ward passes through dead spots. Losing a photograph because of signal is the same failure as losing a citizen's report | Accepted → `internal/api/fieldkit` |
| D058 | **Email/password and Google sign-in are accepted before the public tier; phone verification still gates publication** | Phone OTP needs a DLT-registered sender and a paid SMS route in India. Gating the first working client on a vendor would stall the field kit and the evaluation set, and the thing ADR 0011 protects — published claims about named parties — does not exist yet | Accepted → [ADR 0017](../04-adr/0017-email-and-google-identity-before-public-tier.md) |
| D059 | **One Flutter codebase serves Android and the web** | The same capture surface is needed on a phone in a ward and in a browser for a demo. Two codebases for one screen is the expensive way to get there. The web build deploys to Cloudflare Pages | Accepted → `app/` |
| D060 | **The media insert is idempotent on the content digest, not only the report** | A retried upload re-sends the same bytes. `SaveReport` was already idempotent but `AddReportMedia` was not, so each retry added a row pointing at the same object — inflating media counts and double-counting a photograph in any evaluation export | Accepted → migration `0007_media_idempotency.sql` |
| D061 | **The API runs as a Docker Compose service on the existing Dokploy instance; collection stays on Cloud Run** | Two workloads with different lifecycles: the collector runs twice a day and exits from Mumbai because BMC geo-restricts; the API must stay up and has no geography constraint. Dokploy already provides Traefik, Let's Encrypt and a deploy story | Accepted → [ADR 0018](../04-adr/0018-api-on-dokploy.md) |
| D062 | **The database CA may travel as an environment variable, base64-encoded** | Dokploy's only secret channel is environment variables, and a multi-line PEM crosses two parsers on the way in. The process materialises it to a 0600 file and still connects with verify-full | Accepted → `config.Postgres.CAFile` |
| D063 | **v1 is eight systems, built strictly one at a time, and only one is open at any moment** | The twenty-item build order was accurate and unusable — it read as a queue rather than a sequence of finished things, and working several at once made the state of the project impossible to hold in the head. A system is not started until the one before it meets a bar that can be checked | Accepted → [v0.1 MVP §5](../05-delivery/02-milestone-v0-mvp.md) |
| D064 | **Contract attribution is the first system** | It is the hypothesis: if a citizen does not act differently when told a contract covers the stretch and is under warranty, the roadmap needs replanning. Cheaper to learn now than after three systems are built on the assumption. It is also the only system testable today — it needs a coordinate and the works data, not the classifier or the resolver | Accepted → system 1 |
| D065 | **v1 stays public, in one ward** | Kandivali West, road defects, BMC only, as Phase 1 always specified. Keeping it public keeps phone OTP, redaction, moderation and the public pages inside v1 rather than deferring them into a second scope | Confirmed 28 Sep 2026 |
| D066 | **The API moves to Cloud Run in `asia-south1`, beside the collector** | The shared Dokploy host went unreachable and is not ours to restart — the exact risk [ADR 0018](../04-adr/0018-api-on-dokploy.md) recorded in its own consequences. Cloud Run removes the machine entirely: no patching, no certificate renewal, no neighbour. Measured, the cold-start objection that ruled it out was overstated — 35 ms warm, a couple of seconds once after idle | Accepted → [ADR 0019](../04-adr/0019-api-on-cloud-run.md) |
| D067 | **Health is served on `/v1/health` as well as `/healthz`** | Google's frontend answers `/healthz` itself: on Cloud Run the request never reaches the container and produces no request log, while every other route behaves normally. Anything checking the service from outside needs a path inside our own namespace | Accepted → `internal/api` |
| D068 | **The pilot ward is R/C (Borivali), not R/S (Kandivali West)** | Phase 0's 500 photographs and 200 ground-truth points are gathered on foot, so the ward has to be one the maintainer actually walks through — he lives in Borivali. Independently, measured against the 4,673 works collected, R/C carries 199 road works with 198 geometries, the most of any ward in Mumbai, ahead of H/W (161) and R/S (118). The reachable ward is also the densest one | Accepted → [v0.1 MVP](../05-delivery/02-milestone-v0-mvp.md) |
| D069 | **System 1's precision bar is met on displaced points, and is not yet met on real captures** | 50 of 50 answers judged right by a human, giving precision of at least 92.9% at 95% confidence — the honest claim from a clean run, not "100%". But the reviewer judged from the same map the join used, so this establishes that the join picks a *plausible* road, not a *correct* one. Only a photograph can settle the latter | Accepted → [v0.1 MVP §5](../05-delivery/02-milestone-v0-mvp.md) |
| D070 | **Ward boundaries come from BMC's own `geo/getwardlayer`, not a third-party dataset** | It is the same API the works come from, so the two agree by construction — verified: all 198 Borivali works fall inside BMC's own R/C polygon. | D071 | **Ward codes are normalised on the way in, to the glossary's form** | One BMC API returns both spellings: the works carry `R/C`, the boundary layer carries `RC`. Joining them unnormalised matches nothing, silently, for every report — which looks identical to a ward with no problems in it | Accepted → `internal/jurisdiction` |
| D072 | **The routing gate measures distance to the ward *line*, not to the polygon** | Distance to a polygon is zero anywhere inside it, which says nothing about whether the answer is safe. Nearness to the edge, compared against the position's own error, is what decides whether to route or to ask | Accepted → `Resolve` |
| D073 | **Coverage in Borivali is about half the road network, and the coverage-honesty line is therefore the common case** | Measured against OpenStreetMap: 43% of carriageway within 5 m of BMC contract geometry, 57% within 25 m. Best on the busier roads — secondary 77%, tertiary 69%. "We don't have contract data for this stretch yet" must read as a normal, informative answer rather than an apology, because roughly half of reports will get it | Accepted → [coverage measurement](../01-research/10-contract-coverage-borivali.md) |
| D074 | **The raw length ratio (19.4%) is not the coverage figure and must not be quoted** | OSM splits one street into many ways and maps dual carriageways separately, while BMC publishes one centreline per work. Comparing total lengths compares different things and understates by roughly half | Accepted → same |
| D075 | **BMC is not on the Maharashtra RTI online portal; its RTIs are filed with BMC directly** | Checked 29 Sep 2026: `rtionline.maharashtra.gov.in` names the authorities it serves, lists 26 municipal bodies including Thane, Navi Mumbai, KDMC, Pune and Nagpur, and does not include Brihanmumbai. BMC's own online RTI page times out like most of its portal, so the reliable route is a paper application to the PIO with a court-fee stamp | Accepted → [RTI 1](../06-operations/rti-01-pothole-sla-circulars.md) |
| D076 | **Jurisdiction meets its done bar: 98.17% agreement on 2,405 road works** | BMC's works endpoint states each work's ward; BMC's boundary endpoint decides it. Two independent statements from the same API agreed on 2,361 of 2,405, with no point failing to resolve and only 0.7% flagged for a question. The v0.1 bar is ≥95% | Accepted → `internal/jurisdiction` |
| D077 | **BMC's online RTI form serves 2 of 24 wards; R/C RTIs are filed on paper** | Checked 29 Sep 2026 against the live form: the mandatory Ward dropdown offers only KW Ward and MW Ward. Borivali (R/C) is absent, as are 21 other wards. Combined with [D075](#) — BMC is not on the state portal either — every R/C RTI goes by post or counter, with a court-fee stamp and dated proof of receipt | Accepted → [RTI 1](../06-operations/rti-01-pothole-sla-circulars.md) |
| D078 | **The three pothole circulars are unobtainable without an RTI, and the RTI is now lower priority anyway** | Searched and not found on Indian Kanoon (control-tested), Google, Bing, BMC's circulars page, Wayback's circular index, or the PIL 71/2013 judgments. They exist publicly only as bare citations in 15 of 17 ward RTI manuals. But BMC's own published road tender already gives a citable 24-hour deadline with a ₹5,000/day penalty, so the RTI refines rather than unblocks | Accepted → [SLA sources](../01-research/11-pothole-sla-sources.md) |
| D079 | **Three pothole deadlines exist and the platform must say which is which** | 48 hours binds the corporation (Bombay HC, `2025:BHC-OS:18736-DB` para 70(ix)); 24 hours binds the contractor during the defect liability period (BMC tender ETH_8000040832 §10.11), with ₹5,000 per day per pothole under §7.4; the June 2026 "24 hours" is a verbal instruction at a review meeting with no circular and must not be recorded as a constant. Where two apply the shorter binds, and a citizen reading "24 hours" deserves to know it is the contractor's contract rather than a court order | Accepted → same |
| D080 | **Road ownership is seasonal, and must never be derived from road width** | Outside the monsoon the ward maintains both major and minor roads; during the monsoon major roads and bus routes pass to contractors under central Roads & Traffic. Ch.E (Roads & Traffic) manual ch.2 §2.5(iv). No published width threshold exists — H/West lists a 19.6 m road as minor and 10 m roads as major | Accepted → migration 0016 |
| D081 | **The court's interval is written "forty-eight hours" in words** | Any watcher grepping `48 hours` over the order finds nothing, and paragraph 70(iv) uses the same phrase for an unrelated police duty, so a naive match returns the wrong paragraph. Match on paragraph number and surrounding phrase | Accepted → [SLA sources](../01-research/11-pothole-sla-sources.md) |

| D082 | **Public downloads get their own R2 bucket; the archive is not public** | Public access on R2 is a property of a bucket, not a prefix, so publishing the APK from the archive bucket would publish the citizen photographs stored beside it. Checked 1 Oct 2026: the archive bucket's r2.dev URL was serving a stored capture to anyone with the key, unauthenticated. Disabled, and `tracesarkar-downloads` created for the APK | Accepted → [releasing the app](../06-operations/05-releasing-the-app.md) |
| D083 | **The Android app is signed with a fixed release key, and a build that is not fails CI** | The key's SHA-1 is registered as a Google OAuth Android client, and Android refuses to install an update signed by a different key. A debug-signed build is therefore worse than no build: Google sign-in fails and existing installs are stranded. The workflow stops if the keystore secret is absent and verifies the certificate subject after building | Accepted → `.github/workflows/android.yml` |
