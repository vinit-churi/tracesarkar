**Scope.** Original and repair works on roads, bridges and buildings carried out by the Public Works
Department. It revises DLPs first set by a 1989 circular. **It does not bind BMC**, which sets DLPs
contract by contract. It is a sourced reference point for state PWD works, not a BMC constant.

**How this was read.** The PDF's Marathi text layer is glyph-corrupted by extraction, so the row
labels below are translations. The numbers and the English terms in the table (Flexible Pavement,
Rigid Pavement, MPM, BM, DBM, BC, OGC, SDR/CTR, Special Repairs, Current Repairs, IRC) come through
the text layer intact and are verbatim. A human re-read of the page images is still owed before any

## Paragraph 1 — inspection duties during the DLP (translated)

- The sub-divisional engineer inspects completed works **at least twice a year during the DLP,
  generally before and after the monsoon**.
- Defects found are repaired by the contractor at the time. Where a defect is beyond repair, the
  contractor is told to rebuild and the work is to be done **within three months**. Disputes go to
  the Chief Engineer, whose decision is final and binding on the contractor; the tender is to say
  so.
- **At least three months before the DLP ends**, the executive engineer and deputy engineer jointly
  inspect the work and review the contractor's repairs.
- The superintending engineer pre-inspects repairs every six months for bridges longer than 600 m
  and buildings with a plinth area above 1,000 m².

## Paragraphs 2–3

- Tender forms must carry these DLPs and the inspection and repair duties as clauses.
- All earlier instructions on this subject are superseded.

---

## Why the inspection duties matter

They are record-generating obligations. For any PWD work inside its DLP, an RTI can ask for the
twice-yearly inspection reports and the pre-expiry joint inspection, by date. That is a sharper
question than asking whether the road was repaired.
